From one skill to a valuation

A bottom-up model built from published SARS, National Treasury and market figures. Every number below is either sourced and dated or a dial you can move. Nothing is asserted.

01 · The unit

What one correct answer is worth

Take a single skill — the VAT registration check — and a single moment: a South African business crosses R2 300 000 of taxable supplies and has 21 business days to register.

Miss it and there is no fixed fine. SARS backdates the registration to the date the threshold was breached, and the business then owes output VAT on everything it has sold since — at 15/115 of gross supplies — whether or not it ever charged VAT to a customer. It cannot go back and collect it.

Worked example: R3 000 000 of supplies, registered twelve months late

Backdated output VATR3 000 000 × 15/115 — unrecoverable from customersR391 304
Late payment penalty10% of outstanding tax (SARS VAT 404, Issue 15)R39 130
Interest, 12 months10.25% prescribed rate, effective 1 March 2026R40 109
Exposure from one missed thresholdbefore any understatement penaltyR470 543
And since 1 April 2026 it is worse. The Tax Administration Laws Amendment Act removed the bar on SARS raising an understatement penalty where the error was a bona fide inadvertent mistake. Honest ignorance stopped being a complete defence four months ago. Standard-case understatement penalties run from 10% to 150% of the shortfall.
The asymmetry is the product. MIA answers this question in about 40 milliseconds, for nothing, from constants read off SARS with the date attached. Govchain charges R2 970 to do the registration. A tax practitioner charges R900–R2 000 an hour. The cost of the answer is zero; the cost of the wrong answer is six figures.

A skill is a stateless function over small numbers. It holds no state, queues nothing and calls no model — the calculation endpoint makes no AI request at all. The hundred-thousandth answer costs what the first one did, which is essentially nothing.

05 · The honest finding

South Africa alone does not get there

Run the numbers and the conclusion is uncomfortable, which is why it is worth stating plainly rather than hiding behind a hockey stick.

A $1B valuation at the high-growth multiple of 19.8× needs roughly $51m of revenue — about R858m. At R1 200 per business per year that would take 715 000 paying businesses, which is more than every active VAT vendor in South Africa. The SME layer in one country cannot produce a unicorn at that price point. Push the price to R6 000 a year and it needs 149 000 paying businesses — 30% of every active vendor in the country, at five times the price. Possible on a spreadsheet; nobody has ever done it.

What can get there is one of three things, and an investor is entitled to know which one you are claiming:

The blend is the Strategic Memo's flywheel, with arithmetic under it. The free, open layer is not charity and it is not marketing — it is the input that makes the highest-margin line saleable. Institutions do not buy a calculator. They buy coverage, provenance and the outcome data that only exists because the free layer is being used.

The one assumption everything rests on is that an institution will pay six figures a year for verified, credentialed, continuously-checked regulatory computation. Nothing else in this model is load-bearing in the same way. It is also the cheapest assumption to test — one conversation with one DFI, and it costs nothing but an introduction.

06 · What funding buys

The unit of deployment is a verified skill

With the infrastructure built, a new skill costs roughly ten to fourteen hours of senior work — R9 000 to R12 000, fully verified against primary sources, tested and documented. That is the unit an investor can reason about.

For scale: the average South African venture round in 2023 was R9.54m, and 74.9% of active deals were under R10m. R350 000 is not a venture round. It is roughly six weeks of one senior engineer, and it triples the jurisdictional coverage of the library.

Which is the honest point about the raise. Skills at R10 000 each are not what a seven-figure round is for. The round is for the practitioner verification network, the engine layers, distribution and the outcomes data. The library is the evidence that the team executes — which is what makes funding the rest a reasonable risk rather than a bet on a memo.
